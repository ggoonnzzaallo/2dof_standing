# CAD — centered PTK 7465 MG prototype, millimetres

`assembly.step` includes three original printed parts and simplified purchased-part envelopes. Import STEP into Fusion, SolidWorks, FreeCAD or Onshape. The parametric source is `build_cad.py` (CadQuery; generated and checked using 2.8.0). This is newly constructed geometry, not copied from the reference STL files. This revision centers the battery and both moving links over the base, brings each passive bearing support closer to its servo horn, and uses the same two-sided printed cage at both servos.

The CAD now uses a **PTK 7465-family envelope**, but the only dimensioned illustration located is labeled **7465W MG**; the ordered servo is **7465 MG (non-W)**. The [manufacturer's related MG-D listing](https://www.ptkhobby.com/en/sys-pd/234.html) gives the same 23.9 × 12 × 26.6 mm class and 25T output, while the [dimensioned 7465W illustration](https://manuals.plus/ae/1005008789583991) shows a 23.8 mm body, 12 mm width, 31.8 mm ear span, about 27.8 mm mounting pitch, 22 mm main case depth and 30.1 mm overall height including spline. These are **provisional dimensions**, not a verified drawing of the received non-W units. The horn face, boss and mounting interface still require caliper checks. The generated STEP/STL files are a reviewable starting point, not a print-and-assemble guarantee.

Four disposable test pieces are generated: `servo_mount_fit_coupon.step/.stl` reproduces the complete front-and-rear servo cage; `horn_fit_coupon.step/.stl` reproduces the adapter face, center access and four radial slots; `pivot_fit_coupon.step/.stl` contains a bearing seat and a separate blind pin bore; `battery_tunnel_fit_coupon.step/.stl` is a 22 mm long tunnel cross-section to test pack clearance and roof bridging. Print the relevant coupons after the servos, battery and proposed pivot hardware arrive, and fill the [incoming measurement sheet](FIT_CHECK.md). The coupons are **not** installed on the robot.

STLs retain the assembly coordinates. Use the slicer's place-on-bed/orient tools for each part; they are not prearranged on a print plate. Start with a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, at least 4 walls and 30–40% infill in PLA+ or PETG. Each cage has a front mounting hoop, a rear collar and two 3.5 × 8 mm side rails connecting them; the upper cage is the same geometry rotated 90°. The rear is open for the servo lead, while the side gaps expose the case and mounting fasteners. The lower post and upper cheek have 5 mm axial thickness with tapered roots. Inspect layer direction at cage-to-deck/web joints, disk-to-spine transitions and bearing cheeks. These dimensions improve the load path but have **not** been stress-tested or checked by FEA. Use actual sliced masses in simulation; CAD solid volumes are not printed masses.

The base footprint is 70 × 64 mm; the full upright assembly is approximately **70 × 66 × 145 mm** because the upper servo and rear collar project just past the −Y base edge. Joint origins are `(0,0,42)` and `(0,0,87)` mm; axes +X then +Y at neutral. Both servo assemblies shift 18 mm inward along their output axes, so the moving spine, paddle, battery and joint axes all lie over `(x,y)=(0,0)`. Both cage body openings use **23.9 × 12.0 mm nominal** plus **0.3 mm per-side clearance**; the provisional flange-hole pitch is **27.8 mm**, and the nominal output-axis offset is **6.0 mm** toward one end of the case. These are parameters in `build_cad.py`, not fixed measured truths. Neither SG90 nor FT90M horns are interchangeable with the likely PTK **25T ~5 mm** spline; use the included PTK OEM horns.

The battery slides into the center tunnel from the +X end. Its provisional envelope is 59.5 × 19 × 7.5 mm; the tunnel is 21 mm wide and 10 mm tall above the 3 mm floor, with an end stop at −X. A small cable tie through the two windows at +X retains the pack **at its end**, without squeezing the pouch. The XIAO Sense sits on the +Y low shelf and the Pololu switch on the −Y shelf. This removes the separate battery-guard print. Check the received cell, connector and lead bend radius before final printing; the battery must slide in and out without force. Add insulation under both boards and strain-relieve moving wires.

**Fit-check parameters:** the assumed OEM horn face is 16.5 mm in the original servo-local coordinates; after the 18 mm inward shift, `HORN_FACE=-1.5 mm` in robot coordinates. This is still unmeasured. The now **28 mm diameter, 3 mm thick** printed disk has a 6.8 mm center opening and four radial 2.2 mm slots for a drilled OEM cross/double horn. Use two opposed lateral slots, not the slot under the beam. Each disk has a 12 mm diameter outer hub with a blind 3.05 mm nominal bore for a 3 × 12 mm pin. The fixed cheek has an 8.05 mm nominal bearing pocket, 3 mm deep, with a 6.2 mm rear relief so the shoulder clears the rotating inner race and seats near the outer race. Fit-check horn thickness, slot-to-horn-hole location, screw length, nut/washer clearance, center-screw access, wire exit and spline engagement on physical hardware. Do not print a replacement spline or force a center screw with an unverified thread.

The support is **coaxial** with each servo output: the lower bearing cheek is part of the base and its pin is in the middle link; the upper cheek is part of the middle link and its pin is in the paddle. The pin rotates with its link while the bearing outer ring stays with the parent. This creates a second radial support point across the joint, relieving bending at the servo spline and horn. It is a supported shaft, not a literal double-shear fastener. The upper cheek rises directly from a tapered root on the centered spine, with no long side bridge. The added material changes mass and inertia; use sliced and then measured masses in the simulator.

**Assembly order:** before attaching a horn, slide each servo body into its cage from the output side until the ears meet the front hoop. Route the lead out the open rear; check that its strain relief clears the rear collar without a tight bend. Fasten both ears through the hoop, then install the OEM horn and center screw while there is still straight-on access. The printed outer hub blocks that screwdriver access after assembly, so attach the printed adapter to the horn with its two lateral screws afterward. Seat the bearing by pressing only on its outer ring; if the pocket is loose, retain the **outer ring only** with a small amount of suitable adhesive. Trial-fit the pin to the bearing, then retain the pin in the printed blind hub with a controlled press fit or a tiny amount of suitable adhesive after confirming smooth rotation. Keep adhesive out of the races. The pin should not bottom against the bearing or clamp its inner race axially; trim or change pin length if actual dimensions demand it. Both 3.05 and 8.05 mm printed bores are nominal CAD dimensions, not validated printed fits.

On receipt, measure and record: case length/width/depth; ear span and hole centers/diameters; output-axis offset from the case center; flange face from the back; boss and shaft projection; rear lead and strain-relief envelope; OEM horn arm thickness, radius and hole diameter; OEM center-screw thread/length. Adjust the parameters and rerun the generator before final printing. Check both servos in the full cage coupon: small manufacturing or variant differences matter at a 0.3 mm nominal cavity clearance.

The regenerated **provisional** assembly has no nominal volume intersections in `nominal_clashes.json`. `motion_check.json` checks 81 configurations at 20-degree increments across ±80 degrees, including the outboard bearing and pin envelopes; it does not check a continuous sweep or the real horns, fasteners, leads, ties or print tolerances. Passing this check is not a guarantee of a usable motion range or physical recovery.

The two bearings and pins remain **proposed additions, not included in the user's reported orders**. The centered layout adds no new electrical components. Review it against the delivered PTK horns and battery before buying or printing the full arm. The battery tunnel and wider links add material, so scripted recovery and 1S servo margin still need physical testing.
