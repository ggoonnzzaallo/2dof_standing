**Alternative if joint-angle readback matters, replacing rather than adding to the two FT90Ms:** [FEETECH FT90M-FB at QKits](https://store.qkits.com/rc-robotics/servos/ft90m-fb-micro-size-270-degree-300-degree-2kg-robot-servo-with-feedback.html) was listed in stock at **$8.50 each (USD)**. Its fourth wire exposes an analog angle signal, while the ordinary three servo wires still work as before. This is **not** an SO-100-style smart servo or a proven precision upgrade: it does not provide digital angle/speed/current telemetry, eliminate backlash, or improve the internal positioning loop. The feedback lead can remain insulated and unused for the first policy. [FEETECH specifies](https://www.feetechrc.com/2kg-cm-digital-steering-gear-ft90m-fb.html) 3–8.4 V operation, a 20T spline and a 23.2 × 12 × 25.5 mm case, close to the selected FT90M envelope. Two would make the parts subtotal **$61.85 before shipping/tax**. QKits ships from Canada, and the delivered US price and included horn/screw set have **not** been verified; confirm those before substituting. Do **not** order both pairs merely as insurance. The standard FT90M remains the verified package-with-horns baseline. See the [servo comparison](docs/SERVO_DECISION.md) and [optional feedback wiring plan](HARDWARE.md#what-the-robot-can-measure).

**No capacitor or signal resistors are required for the first build.** Wire XIAO D0/D1 directly to the servo signal leads, with a shared ground. Keep battery-to-switch-to-servo power wiring short and perform the [loaded power/brownout check](HARDWARE.md#power-budget-and-brownout-check) before untethered runs. The XIAO's 3V3 and VBUS pins must not carry servo current. If brownouts or servo noise appear, investigate the measured battery and 3V3 rails before adding the optional capacitor below. If a particular servo back-powers from its signal wire while its power is off, first fix firmware power sequencing; add series resistors only after confirming they solve that measured issue.

**Fewest-parts experiment:** omitting the Pololu switch too leaves the XIAO Sense, two servos, battery, mating lead, ties and fasteners: **$58.34** at the listed USA prices. In that configuration, servos remain electrically connected whenever the pack is plugged in; firmware cannot disconnect their power. Unplug the battery for a complete shutdown, and do not assume USB charging is safe with the servo rail connected and active. The [controller decision](docs/CONTROLLER_DECISION.md) explains the tradeoff. Rev A keeps the switch so the MCU can disable the servos.

If needed: [Adafruit 1649 heat-shrink assortment](https://www.adafruit.com/product/1649), **$4.95**, makes the recommended purchase subtotal **$67.78**. Heat shrink is insulating tubing that tightens around soldered joints when heated, preventing exposed wires from touching and shorting. Use approximately 15 short sleeves, typically 2–3 mm unshrunk for individual joints and larger sizes for the power splice. Existing heat shrink saves a separate vendor order.

The standard FT90M spline is **20-tooth, approximately 3.95 mm**, not a standard-size 25T spline. Use the matching horns shown in the servo package photo. Their actual hole positions and stack height remain a CAD fit check. Drill two suitable horn holes to 2.2 mm to match the adapter slots; trim unused horn arms if needed. Do not drill the spline hub. The hardware pack includes 17 spare complete M2 sets before any center-screw fallback. Split lock washers are supplied but not required against the printed plastic.

## Materials already assumed at an equipped bench

These are explicit exclusions from the purchased-parts subtotal, not hidden robot components:

- Approximately 35–45 g PLA+/PETG including support/waste. Four robot prints: base, middle link, paddle, battery guard.
- Solder and flux; approximately 15 heat-shrink sleeves if already owned.
- Two small thin PET/Kapton insulating shims for PCB undersides, plus a small amount of insulating tape for exposed joints. Mounting must remain firm, not on thick soft foam.
- Wiring: supplied RCY pigtail plus shortened servo leads. Reuse servo-lead offcuts for controller BAT/GND and signal connections. Keep main battery/servo power connections short; use the supplied 20-AWG wire for the battery trunk. Allow moving service loops.
- USB-C **data** cable and ordinary USB 5 V source for programming/charging; laptop, printer, soldering tools, calipers, **DC multimeter**, three thin temporary insulated voltage-sense wires and two clips, small Phillips driver, 2.2 mm drill. The sense wires let you measure BAT and 3V3 without touching moving hardware; see [the procedure](HARDWARE.md#power-budget-and-brownout-check). A scale, current-limited bench supply and two-channel oscilloscope are useful for further characterization, but not first-purchase robot components.

Do not purchase a Raspberry Pi, PCA9685, separate IMU, buck/boost module, or separate LiPo charger for this architecture. Charge the connected battery through the XIAO's USB-C port with its charger set to approximately 100 mA and the servo rail disabled by firmware. Optional smaller batteries should be chosen only after confirming dimensions, connector, discharge rating and standard 4.2 V charge chemistry.
